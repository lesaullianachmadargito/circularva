Home / Our Farm

From rejected salak to dried larvae

Our farm sits in Sleman, Yogyakarta, among the snake fruit orchards. Here is how each batch is made.

Step 1

Collect rejected salak

Salak that farmers cannot sell is collected from local farmer groups and chopped into feed for the larvae.

Step 2

Feed the larvae

Black soldier fly larvae feed on the fruit in trays and bioponds until they reach harvest size.

Step 3

Rear under monitoring

Trays sit on racks inside a screened hall. Sensor nodes log temperature, humidity, CO₂ and ammonia to a dashboard.

Step 4

Harvest and separate

Larvae are sieved from the residue. The residue becomes frass for the farms.

Step 5

Dry, pack and code

Larvae are dried, packed in sealed pouches or bags, and given a batch code that ties the pack to its harvest.

Quality and traceability

Plant-based diet

The larval feed is fruit and plant material only, which is also the rule the strictest import markets apply.

Batch coding

A batch code on every pack links it to its harvest date and rearing log, so any question about a pack can be traced back.

Food-safety training

Quality is run by a lead trained in HACCP, GMP, ISO 22000 and FSSC 22000.

The loop closes on the farm

Salak farms send their rejected fruit to the larvae. The larvae become feed. The frass left behind goes back to the salak farms as a soil amendment.

About CircuLarva

A village farm with an export plan

CircuLarva started as a way to use the salak that farmers in Sleman throw away. We built a black soldier fly farm with the village, turned the fruit into larvae, and now pack those larvae for buyers outside Indonesia.

The team comes from Universitas Gadjah Mada and works with the farmer groups that supply the fruit and run the rearing site.
